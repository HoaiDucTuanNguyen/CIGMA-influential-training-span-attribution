# CIGMA-influential-training-span-attribution
Deep neural NLP models are widely used for their strong predictive performance, but their black-box nature makes explanation, including training-data attribution, essential in high-stakes settings. However, existing training-data attribution methods still fall short in explaining span-level neural NLP predictions, where variable-length prediction spans make explanations more challenging to ground in the deployed model’s decision behavior and to distinguish supportive from misleading training evidence. We present CIGMA (Counterfactual Influence via Gold-Margin Ablation), the first post-hoc framework for explaining span-level neural NLP predictions via counterfactual training-data attribution. CIGMA combines decision-aligned training-span evidence, adaptive retrieval of candidate training spans, and a lightweight counterfactual scoring procedure to estimate their supportive-vs-misleading influence at the span level. To strengthen trust in the resulting explanations, the framework is paired with a behavior-linked evaluation protocol covering explanatory quality, sensitivity and stability, statistical reliability, external sanity checks, human-facing evaluation, and transferability. Experiments on biomedical data show that CIGMA produces behavior-linked span-level explanations, and that sensitivity analyses, component ablations, deletion-and-retraining and data-cleaning sanity checks, and a pilot human evaluation support the robustness and practical relevance of its design choices. Transfer experiments further provide encouraging evidence of transferability across domains and span-level NLP tasks.
